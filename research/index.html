Florence · Self-Guided Research

Wine & Gelato Walks

Three walkable Florence routes. Every option includes three wine stops and three gelato stops, with a specific tasting target and a reason each stop earns its place.

Choose the Florence you want that day.

These are intentionally self-guided: start when the weather and family energy cooperate, skip a stop without losing the route, and order one small pour or cup at each location.

Walk I

Historic Center Classics

The easiest first-time route: Duomo lanes, Piazza della Signoria, Santa Croce and the Arno. It prioritizes historic institutions and classic Tuscan wine styles.

Approx. 2.4 km · 2.5–3.5 hours
Best start: 3:30–4:30 PM
Open route
1

Perché No!

Gelato
Via dei Tavolini 19r

Try: pistachio or the best seasonal fruit sorbet. This 1939 shop is a useful quality baseline: natural color, compact display, and restrained sweetness.

2

Cantina Barbagianni

Wine
Via Sant'Egidio 13r

Try: Vernaccia di San Gimignano. Its citrus, almond and mineral profile gives the route a fresh opening before the deeper Sangiovese pours.

3

Gelateria dei Neri

Gelato
Via dei Neri 9/11r

Try: dark chocolate plus a fruit flavor, or ask for the house specialty that day. The contrast shows whether each flavor stays distinct rather than merely tasting sweet.

4

Enoteca Pitti Gola e Cantina

Wine
Piazza de' Pitti 16

Try: Chianti Classico Riserva. This is the route's serious Sangiovese stop—cherry, herbs, acidity and savory structure rather than a generic “house red.”

5

Gelateria La Carraia

Gelato
Piazza Nazario Sauro 25r

Try: Delizia Carraia (white chocolate with pistachio sauce), cantuccini, or ricotta and pear. These are confirmed house flavors and make this the indulgent stop.

6

Le Volpi e l'Uva

Wine
Piazza dei Rossi 1

Try: ask for a small-production Tuscan red that is not Chianti—ideally Montecucco Sangiovese or a native-grape discovery. The by-the-glass list changes, which is the point.

Best for: a first afternoon in Florence. Stop after #4 for the compact version; continue across the river for the strongest finish.
Walk II

Oltrarno Artisan Loop

A slower neighborhood route through Santo Spirito and San Frediano, pairing artisan gelato with wine bars that feel more local than checklist-driven.

Approx. 2.8 km · 3–4 hours
Best start: 4:00–5:00 PM
Open route
1

Sbrino Gelatificio Contadino

Gelato
Via dei Serragli 32r

Try: a dairy-forward flavor such as fior di latte or a seasonal fruit flavor. This is the “ingredient clarity” stop—simple choices make quality easier to judge.

2

Il Santo Bevitore

Wine
Via di Santo Spirito 64/66r

Try: a Tuscan white or skin-contact wine by the glass. It broadens the tasting beyond red wine and works well before another gelato stop.

3

La Sorbetteria

Gelato
Piazza Tasso 11r

Try: lemon-and-sage, ginger, or another herbal seasonal flavor when offered. This stop is about creative combinations that still taste clean and refreshing.

4

Enoteca Spontanea

Wine
Via Maggio 61r

Try: a low-intervention Sangiovese or native Tuscan grape recommended by the staff. Ask for something savory and food-friendly, not merely “natural.”

5

Gelateria La Carraia

Gelato
Piazza Nazario Sauro 25r

Try: cantuccini or Schiacciata Fiorentina for a distinctly Tuscan dessert profile. Use the smallest cup; this route already has plenty of richness.

6

Le Volpi e l'Uva

Wine
Piazza dei Rossi 1

Try: a staff-selected flight of two half pours: one Tuscan and one from another Italian region. It turns the final stop into a comparison rather than another full glass.

Best for: a relaxed adults-and-kids wander. Santo Spirito provides piazzas and breathing room between tastings.
Walk III

Market to Santa Croce

The most food-curious option, beginning near San Lorenzo and finishing near Santa Croce. Choose this for broader Tuscan wine styles and more distinctive gelato flavors.

Approx. 3.1 km · 3–4 hours
Best start: 2:30–4:00 PM
Open route
1

My Sugar

Gelato
Via de' Ginori 49r

Try: pistachio or a seasonal special. This compact artisan shop is a strong opening because the flavors tend to be focused rather than theatrically displayed.

2

Vino Tasting Global

Wine
Via del Gomitolo dell'Oro 11r

Try: a three-part Tuscan comparison in small pours—Vernaccia, Chianti Classico and a Bolgheri blend—when available. It gives the clearest educational overview.

3

Perché No!

Gelato
Via dei Tavolini 19r

Try: one nut flavor and one fruit flavor. The pairing makes texture, roasting and freshness easy to compare without ordering a large portion.

4

Procacci

Wine
Via de' Tornabuoni 64r

Try: a glass of Tuscan sparkling wine or a crisp white with the signature truffle panino. This is the polished, historic aperitivo stop rather than a formal tasting.

5

Gelateria dei Neri

Gelato
Via dei Neri 9/11r

Try: ricotta-based, fig, or caramel if available. The goal is a richer Tuscan-style combination after the lighter opening cups.

6

Enoteca Pinchiorri area / Santa Croce wine stop

Wine
Santa Croce neighborhood

Try: finish at a nearby enoteca with a Brunello or Vino Nobile half pour. Ask the server to explain how it differs from Chianti Classico before ordering.

Best for: the most varied tasting. Because the last wine list can change, confirm the final enoteca and opening hours on the day of the walk.
